About Black History Hub

A proof of concept built for the Black Cultural Centre for Nova Scotia at HubHacks 2026.

The problem

Records of Black history in Canada are spread across museums, archives, libraries, universities, governments and community organizations. Each one has its own catalogue, terms and access rules, so people often need to know which institution to search before they can even start. Related records held in different places are hard to connect.

What this does (and doesn't)

Black History Hub is a discovery layer. You can search descriptions of records from many institutions in one place, and each result sends you to the institution that holds or describes it.

It does not host, copy or replace any collection. The original institution stays the authority on its records, and every result links back to its own page.

How it works

  1. 1

    Collect

    We gather descriptive information (metadata) about records from each institution and check the links by hand.

  2. 2

    Organize

    Each description is mapped into one shared structure: title, dates, people, places, subjects, type, holder and source.

  3. 3

    Search & link back

    You search everything at once, and each result links to the institution's own record.

Our principles

  • Holder and source are kept separate. The institution that holds an original is not always the one that describes it, and every record says which is which.
  • Provenance on every record: where the information came from, when it was added and how it was collected.
  • Verification dates: each record shows when its details and link were last checked.
  • Rights and access restrictions are respected. We store descriptions and links, not copies of images or documents.
  • No invented information. Any AI-assisted search will only cite real records in the hub. If something can't be verified, we don't present it as fact.

The 41 records are described by 16 institutions; the rest of the 21 hold originals or are cited in our notes. Just over half relate to Nova Scotia, and the others cover Quebec, Ontario, New Brunswick, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta and British Columbia. There are no records yet for Prince Edward Island, Newfoundland and Labrador, or the territories.

This is a proof of concept. If you don't find something here, that doesn't mean it doesn't exist. Try the catalogues of the institutions directly.
