A Love Letter to Africville

February 2021

A 96-page book by Amanda Carvery-Taylor, whose father grew up in Africville. It gathers former residents' memories and photographs to show the community's family life, childhood, nightlife and Elders' lessons, not only its hardships.

Africville and the Dynamics of State Power in Postwar Canada

2010-06-06

Uses the relocation of Africville's residents during Halifax's mid-1960s urban renewal program to examine how postwar state power actually worked. Argues that the case reveals more than racism alone, including the ideas of the good life built into the welfare state.

Journal article Online

The View from Jacob Street: Reframing Urban Renewal in Postwar Halifax

2019-12-20

Looks at Halifax redevelopment between Gordon Stephenson's 1957 study and the 1964 start of the Africville relocation, focusing on the scale of displacement and how race and class shaped who was moved. Places Africville within the city's wider urban-renewal program.

Remembering Africville: Source Guide

A Halifax Municipal Archives guide to city records about Africville, such as council minutes, relocation compensation case files, building permits, photographs and maps, with archival reference numbers. Many of the listed records are digitized; some can be seen only in person or once personal information has been redacted.
